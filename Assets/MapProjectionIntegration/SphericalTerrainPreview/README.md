# Spherical Terrain Preview

Open `Scenes/Spherical Terrain Preview.unity` and press Play. The scene can also be rebuilt or opened from **Window → Map Projection → Spherical Terrain Preview**.

This is an independent presentation scene. Its terrain is generated over the existing `IcoSphere/Resources/Bin/pack_arr_5.bytes` dual grid: 590,492 actual cells, including 12 pentagons. It does not bend the planar HexGrid mesh and does not change gameplay cell IDs, map files, save formats, or the original game scene.

## Controls

- Mouse wheel: damped zoom around the pointer.
- WASD / arrow keys: distance-scaled spherical movement with input damping.
- Q / E: rotate the camera around its geographic focus.
- Drag: rotate the globe; click a cell to select its real spherical polygon.
- Presets: Earth, Europe, East Asia, the Kunlun / Tibetan plateau edge, coast, river, and a pentagon.
- Grid toggle: display actual hexagon and pentagon edges.
- Sun slider: change the elevation of the scene's Directional Light.

## Existing art reused

The current `Default Hex Terrain Style` supplies the near-terrain profile, its authored height/shape atlas, ground/rock/snow/desert albedo and material-response arrays, and its vegetation recipes. Existing tree meshes and their albedo, cutout, normal and translucency materials are retained. Ocean/river resources are read from the current `Water.mat` and `Hex Relief.mat`.

`SphericalWorld` samples map attributes geographically, then generates terrain on the **sphere's** shared corners and edges. Its rivers are 26 newly authored source-to-mouth strokes in spherical longitude/latitude coordinates. Each stroke is routed continuously on the actual sphere corner graph, keeping inland sections on dry corners and ending in the actual coastal blend. Routes cannot branch, cross another route's corner or form a loop. Flat river masks are counted for diagnostics only; they do not generate these routes. `RiverRoutes` retains each complete ordered path, and `OmittedRiverRoutes` explicitly reports a route that the selected graph resolution cannot resolve. These are simplified art routes for the preview, not a GIS reconstruction.

`SphericalSurface` is the shared radial height field for geometry, planting and picking. It blends a compact neighborhood of real cells; shape stamp orientation and irregular polygon shape are independent. `SphericalMountainRanges` ports authored peak anchors, connected ridge bodies and flank detail. `SphericalLandformArt` follows the current flat `HexNearTerrainSurface` rules for encoded rotation, density-dependent peak/footprint variation and the lower ridge stamp. Peak anchors and the connected ridge body use the same varied scale. Every fine polygon uses the same edge subdivision count. Sea and river geometry are spherical as well.

Near materials now aggregate all contributing stamps rather than select one winning stamp or interpolate a discrete biome ID. The CPU snapshots the existing independent land-material mask atlas and accumulates its standard mountain top/snow IDs and three desert stripe IDs plus sand. The shader follows the flat material's source-height blending, per-biome upper layers and highland weathering. Snow retention is concentrated within 0.20–0.65 cell radii of authored peaks; connected saddles retain only the flat recipe's 6% trace. Texture projections remain fixed in planet space and use the local radial normal to distinguish ground from steep faces.

Mountain geometry uses 94% of the existing authored peak/ridge height. Isolated peaks receive a further 12–18% height increase with a tighter footprint, restoring a taller individual-mountain silhouette while retaining the existing imported shape detail and grounded feet. Its rock/snow/strata decisions use a separate `Sample.MaterialRelief`: mountain contributions are evaluated at authored material height, while plain and hill amplitudes retain their existing recipes. The mountain-height correction follows the same coast and river-carve masks before reaching the material. Terrain mesh UV1.y carries this material height; terrain UV1.z retains actual local height. Water uses its own UV1.zw shoreline-slope payload.

Desert dunes call the current flat `HexNearDesertDunes.Evaluate` implementation directly, retaining its windward ramp, short slipface, meandering crests and subsidiary sand ribs. The sphere supplies a continuous periodic longitude/latitude domain and the current profile's wind, wavelength and irregularity. Raised desert platforms use this same dune recipe, with dune amplitude reduced smoothly over the platform shoulder to avoid crests cutting across the transition. This is the project's procedural dune recipe rather than newly imported individual dune models.

The distant surface uses the previous preview's offline satellite color and ETOPO textures. Near art blends into this image between altitudes 220 and 650; far coastlines use the image's geographic coastline rather than coarse mesh triangles. The camera always views the sphere.

`SphericalTerrainStreaming` partitions the real cells into stable geographic tiles. Up to six CPU workers generate nearby tiles; Unity mesh uploads remain on the main thread. Complete tiles appear progressively over the continuous whole-Earth surface. A shared R8 coverage texture synchronizes terrain, ocean and river visibility; a one-neighbor geometry guard covers its filtered edge. The coverage texture and tangent basis are published together on location changes. Obsolete work is cancelled between cells, and cache hits do not rebuild terrain.

The GPU cache is limited to 40 tiles / 512 MiB of estimated payload, evicting inactive tiles first. A currently visible region may exceed the tile-count limit around an octahedral partition seam; its actual byte size remains the relevant memory measure. Mesh CPU copies are released after validation/upload. A bounded temporary cell cache shares guard geometry between concurrent workers and is cleared when generation finishes. Instanced trees reuse the existing grove, glade, forest-edge, canopy, species and tint recipes, with frustum culling, distance LOD and limited shadow range.

`SphericalMeshNormals` welds normals by exact cell/corner/subdivision keys, avoiding repeated numerical height derivatives for each mesh vertex. Every published polygon has a full neighbor guard, so adjacent tiles retain the same incident faces. The river and mountain graphs use a mixed 64-bit edge hash: the default packed-integer XOR hash caused extreme collision chains around East Asia.

`SphericalRiverNetwork` rounds the new main trunks' degree-two joins with common cubic tangents. Both the river ribbon and carved bed evaluate the same curve; each source and mouth keeps its actual graph anchor. Each shared river edge has one stable owning cell across streaming tiles, and outward semicircular endpoint caps avoid overlapping the adjoining ribbon. River water is evaluated at each point's regional platform +0.022 map units, and the central bed at platform −0.32; the ordinary land datum is platform +0.178. A plateau therefore carries a shallow river that follows its local shoulder. Mountain and hill relief eases into the river valley over 1.2–5.2 map units before the narrow channel is carved; dune suppression retains the existing riverbank recipe. Water's continuous dry-land density is accumulated in the existing terrain neighborhood. Shoreline slope and its validity flag are supplied in water UV1.zw so the water shader can resolve a consistent shore-distance band, with detailed, broken foam and layered wash instead of a solid white polygon strip.

`SphericalPlateauField` supplies a regional base independently of local terrain. Existing filtered ETOPO elevation is sampled with a C2-continuous B-spline and converted to one raised platform above ordinary ground. Its height is 1.2 times the profile's plateau height: 4.32 map units with the current profile. A smooth shoulder spans at least eight map units, keeping the raised region legible while blending into ordinary terrain. Shared welded normals continue across the entire shoulder; triangles are no longer split into separate riser normal or material islands. Source landform 3 retains its source value and climate category but uses the appropriate plain or dune recipe over this regional base. The highland preset at 83.90°E, 35.97°N shows the single platform and its surrounding mountains.

The preview clones the active URP asset in memory, selecting a 4096 main-light shadow map and high-quality soft shadows. Shadow distance follows the viewing distance within a 180–600 map-unit range, retaining more shadow-map detail at close terrain views. It restores the previous pipeline when unloaded. The saved project pipeline and material assets are not edited.

Outside the authored map's approximately 63°S–70°N range, the preview uses the existing ETOPO-derived polar land mask and snow material. These are display cells without new gameplay data.

## Implementation limits

The source art and algorithms are reused on a different cell topology; this is not a cell-for-cell copy of the planar map. Individual peak locations, river routing and forest distributions follow the actual spherical cells. Desert strata use the existing classified masks and extend uncovered connecting flanks with the flat material's height bands. The regional platform is derived from geographic elevation, not a newly editable gameplay height layer. Source settlement/farm/special-cell attributes can guide vegetation clearance, but roads, city rendering, units, pathfinding and map-editing brushes are not migrated. Existing texture/profile assets provide the art data; the sphere has its own geometry, shader adaptation, sampling and streaming implementation, so identical frame rates or identical screenshots are not assumed. This revision restores individual-mountain proportions using the existing imported mountain shapes; it does not import new Steam resources.

Fine-ocean guard geometry currently overlaps between adjacent published tiles. Feathered water can therefore composite more than once, and the outer guard's incomplete normal neighborhood can produce a different shoreline slope. Coast brightness and foam seams remain an item for Unity visual review; the river ownership fix does not remove this separate ocean-guard overlap.

## Verification

`Tools/SphericalTerrainPreview/validate_world.py --recursion 5` executes the actual topology/data loader against the existing binary map in an independent math host. Unity's **Validate and Capture** command checks native 1080p rendering across 20 geographic views, including the isolated mountain, ordinary and desert plateau edges, near coast, the reported desert location, date-line views and north pole. It also checks pentagon seams, elevated landforms, sun, selection, zoom, keyboard navigation, rapid region changes, warm cache returns and shaders. The final 2026-09-13 run passed 201/201 checks with no Unity or shader errors; its report is `Artifacts/SphericalTerrainPreview/Validation-revision-0913-validate3.json`. Actual captures and CPU reports are linked from `Artifacts/SphericalTerrainPreview/Revision20260913/Review.html`. Headless CPU results remain separate from measured Unity rendering, loading and frame times; numerical checks do not certify visual style parity with another game.

The current terrain audit is `validate_single_plateau.py`, with its report at `Artifacts/SphericalTerrainPreview/Revision20260913/SinglePlateau/SinglePlateau.json`. It compiles the current world, surface and normal implementations and checks the one-platform height bound, continuous shoulders, real raster seams, polar continuity, shared normals, dunes, mountain proportions and plateau rivers. `validate_picking.py` extracts the actual `TryPick` method and compares near-terrain ray hits with an independent fine first-crossing search; its report is under `Revision20260913/Picking`.

`validate_landform_parity.py` and `LandformParity/Landform-Flat-Parity.json` are historical flat-map comparison baselines. Their former mountain scale and multiple-plateau assumptions do not certify the current revision. The legacy `validate_world.py --surface` height assertions also predate the single-platform change; use `validate_single_plateau.py` for current surface validation. Existing baseline host helpers are reused by the newer tools, which compile the current runtime sources.

For the 2026-09-13 river redraw, `validate_spherical_river_routes.py` compares the saved pre-revision world loader with the current implementation. The R5 graph audit records 26 complete routes, 4,041 edges, 52 endpoints, no branches, no cycles and no omitted routes; every route starts on dry terrain and reaches a coastal mouth. The shortest route has 66 edges. The previous spherical graph had 37,423 edges, 356 connected components and 2,971 branch nodes. Reports are under `Artifacts/SphericalTerrainPreview/Revision20260913/Rivers`. The curve audit also checks shared positions, tangents and riverbed queries. These are CPU validations; they do not certify Unity rendering or the visual result of the full revision.

`validate_river_mesh_ownership.py` executes the extracted current `AddRivers`, `AddRiverWaterVertex` and tile partition methods over the real R5 graph. Its `Revision20260913/RiverMeshOwnership/After.json` report records 4,041 emitted ribbons for 4,041 edges, 52 endpoint caps, no duplicate edges or caps and no degenerate triangles. The pre-fix renderer emitted 4,693 ribbons, including 652 repeated edges and 13 repeated caps. This harness supplies constant surface attributes to isolate ownership and geometry; real river depth, water compositing and ocean guards require the Unity captures.
